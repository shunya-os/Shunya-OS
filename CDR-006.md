# I. Constitutional Definition

*This section quotes the relevant Constitutional definitions. It describes what the Constitution requires. It does not reference implementation, infer from repository evidence, or change constitutional meaning. It is the architectural destination.*

---

### Constitutional Definition 1: Reality (Article II)

**§2.1 The Referent Principle:** Every object, fact, and relationship in the system SHALL refer to something in reality. The referent is primary; the representation is secondary.

**§2.4 Timeline Primacy:** The timeline is the fundamental ordering principle of reality within the system. Every event SHALL be recorded with a monotonically increasing timestamp, a causal parent, the actor that produced it, and the evidence chain it participates in.

**§2.5 Observation and Fallibility:** An observation is a recorded perception of an event. Observations SHALL carry a confidence score, source identifier, timestamp, and supporting evidence.

The Constitution defines Reality as the source of truth outside the system. Within the system, Reality is represented through events on an immutable, chronological timeline. Observations of Reality are provisional and carry confidence scores.

### Constitutional Definition 2: Event Architecture (Article V, §5.5)

All state changes SHALL be communicated through typed events. Events SHALL be:
- Immutable after publication
- Chronologically ordered
- Causally traceable
- Governance-checked before delivery

The Constitution requires typed events for all state changes, with immutability, chronological ordering, causal traceability, and governance enforcement.

### Constitutional Definition 3: Canonical Source Rule (Article VI, §6.1)

Every concept SHALL have exactly one authoritative definition. All other representations SHALL derive from this canonical source.

### Constitutional Definition 4: No-Duplication Rule (Article VI, §6.2)

No concept SHALL be defined twice. Where duplication is discovered, it SHALL be resolved to a single canonical representation.

# III. Architectural Inference

*This section contains conclusions derived from Repository Evidence. Every inference explicitly references supporting evidence. Inferences are not constitutional truth — they remain open to challenge.*

---

### Inference 1: Reality is a continuously changing business projection

**Supporting evidence:** E6 (living-store polls `/api/v1/reality` every 15s). E7 (insights piggyback on same response). Response contains events with timestamps and importance scores.

**Conclusion:** The Reality endpoint appears to represent the canonical continuously changing state of business operations. It has event-like characteristics (timestamps, importance, actor attribution) consistent with §2.4's timeline requirements.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM.

### Inference 2: Three workloads have continuous-event characteristics

**Supporting evidence:** E2 (15s, cursor-based), E6 (15s, reality), E7 (15s, piggyback). Cursor-based fetching implies incremental/delta delivery.

**Conclusion:** These three consumers share a common architectural pattern: they fetch deltas or state snapshots on a sub-minute cadence. This is the defining characteristic of Continuous Reality.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

### Inference 3: Five workloads are adequately served by infrequent polling

**Supporting evidence:** E1 (45s), E3 (30s), E8 (60s), E9 (15s), E5 (in-process 5s). Payloads are summary counts, dashboard snapshots, inference output.

**Conclusion:** These consumers fetch data that changes on a human-perceptible timescale. Push delivery would not improve founder experience.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

### Inference 4: Two workloads are user-triggered

**Supporting evidence:** E4 fetches list on toggle. E10 is dev-only. E11 is opt-in.

**Conclusion:** These consumers fetch data in direct response to user action, not on a timer.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

### Inference 5: The event bus is the canonical publish/subscribe layer but lacks streaming transport

**Supporting evidence:** Only E1 and E2 emit to the bus (after CEP-003). No SSE/WebSocket/push transport exists. Every consumer that needs backend data must poll.

**Conclusion:** The bus handles in-process dispatch but has no streaming transport. Data freshness depends on polling frequency.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

# IV. Constitutional Recommendation

*Every recommendation uses the discipline: "The Constitution defines [concept] as... Repository assessment indicates the current implementation is [Partial/Full/None]." Two independent measurements (Confidence, Constitutional Conformance) are reported. Every recommendation concludes with a Constitutional Gap statement.*

---

### Recommendation 1: Establish the Reality Runtime as the canonical event source for continuously changing business state

The Constitution defines **Reality (Article II)** as the source of truth outside the system, represented through events on an immutable chronological timeline with causal traceability. The **Event Architecture (§5.5)** requires typed, immutable, chronologically ordered, causally traceable events for all state changes. The **Canonical Source Rule (§6.1)** requires exactly one authoritative definition.

**Repository assessment:** The current implementation is **Partial**. `living-store.ts` polls `GET /api/v1/reality` every 15s and stores events transiently in Zustand. The event bus exists (CEP-003) but Reality events bypass it. No immutability, no causal tracing, no chronological ordering for Reality data.

| Recommendation | Confidence | Constitutional Conformance |
|---------------|------------|---------------------------|
| Establish Reality Runtime as canonical event source | MEDIUM | Partial |

**Constitutional Gap:**

> **Current implementation:**
> Reality events arrive via 15s polling, stored transiently in `living-store.ts` Zustand. No bus integration, no immutability, no causal chain.
>
> **Required constitutional state:**
> Reality events are typed, immutable, chronologically ordered, causally traceable, communicated through the canonical event bus (§5.5, §2.4).
>
> **Gap remaining:**
> 15s polling window (events lost between polls); no immutability; no causal traceability; no bus integration for Reality events.
>
> **CEP objective:**
> Replace 15s reality polling with a streaming transport that publishes typed Reality events to the canonical bus, satisfying §5.5 (typed events, chronological ordering) and §2.4 (timeline primacy).

---

### Recommendation 2: SSE is the constitutionally indicated transport for Continuous Reality workloads

The **Event Architecture (§5.5)** requires chronological ordering and causal traceability — properties that SSE provides natively through ordered event delivery. **Founder Experience** (the highest constitutional layer) benefits from continuous awareness, which push delivery enables and polling inherently limits.

**Repository assessment:** The current implementation is **None**. E2 (delta sync, 15s) and E6 (reality, 15s) both poll. No push transport exists.

**Alternative interpretations considered:**
1. *WebSocket instead of SSE* — rejected. Unidirectional server→client matches the event stream pattern. WebSocket adds bidirectional complexity (SSE is simpler and constitutionally sufficient).
2. *Shorter polling intervals* — rejected. 15s already taxes the Reality endpoint per request. Reducing to 5s would triple load without achieving real-time.
3. *Service Worker push* — rejected. Adds PWA complexity with no constitutional benefit over SSE.

| Recommendation | Confidence | Constitutional Conformance |
|---------------|------------|---------------------------|
| SSE for Continuous Reality workloads | MEDIUM | None |

**Constitutional Gap:**

> **Current implementation:**
> E2 and E6 poll every 15s, losing events between polls. No chronological ordering guarantee for Reality snapshots. No causal traceability.
>
> **Required constitutional state:**
> Events arrive in chronological order with causal parent tracking (§2.4, §5.5). Founder perceives continuous awareness rather than batched updates.
>
> **Gap remaining:**
> Backend SSE endpoint not designed. Reality Engine streaming capability unknown. Frontend SSE runtime not built.
>
> **CEP objective:**
> Deliver Continuous Reality events via SSE, achieving chronological ordering and sub-second delivery for the 3 Continuous Reality workloads.

---

### Recommendation 3: Maintain polling for Periodic Observation workloads

The **Simplicity Rule (§4, Pattern Language)** states that the simplest correct solution is the canonical solution. Polling is simpler than push when the data characteristics do not require push.

**Repository assessment:** The current implementation is **Full**. E1 (45s, AI presence), E3 (30s, notification count), E8 (60s, dashboard), E9 (15s, background jobs) all poll at intervals appropriate to their data change frequency.

| Recommendation | Confidence | Constitutional Conformance |
|---------------|------------|---------------------------|
| Maintain polling for Periodic Observation | HIGH | Full |

**Constitutional Gap:** None. Current implementation meets constitutional requirements for Periodic Observation.

---

### Recommendation 4: Keep User-initiated workloads as on-demand fetch

The **Simplicity Rule** applies equally here. On-demand fetch is constitutionally correct for user-triggered actions.

**Repository assessment:** Current implementation is **Full**. E4 fetches on toggle. E10 is dev-only. E11 is opt-in.

| Recommendation | Confidence | Constitutional Conformance |
|---------------|------------|---------------------------|
| Keep on-demand fetch for User-initiated | HIGH | Full |

**Constitutional Gap:** None.
