# Purpose

Trust cannot exist without security.

Security cannot exist without governance.

Governance cannot exist without accountability.

This book defines how SHUNYA preserves organizational trust.

---

# Security Philosophy

Security exists to protect people.

Not inconvenience them.

Every security decision should maximize:

Trust.

Clarity.

Recovery.

Accountability.

Minimal friction.

---

# Trust Model

Trust is earned through:

Correctness.

Consistency.

Transparency.

Recoverability.

Explainability.

Availability.

Trust is never assumed.

It is continuously reinforced.

---

# Zero Trust Principle

Every request is evaluated.

Every action is authenticated.

Every permission is verified.

No component automatically trusts another component.

Trust is continuously validated.

---

# Identity

Every actor possesses identity.

Actors include:

Users.

Organizations.

Artificial Intelligence.

Automations.

Integrations.

APIs.

Services.

Every action remains attributable.

Anonymous business activity should not exist.

---

# Authentication

Authentication verifies identity.

Supported mechanisms include:

Password.

Passkeys.

Multi-Factor Authentication.

Single Sign-On.

Identity Providers.

API Keys.

Service Accounts.

Authentication should evolve without architectural change.

---

# Authorization

Authorization determines capability.

Permissions should answer:

Who?

May perform what?

On which object?

Under which conditions?

For how long?

Authorization is object-centric.

Not screen-centric.

---

# Least Privilege

Users receive the minimum permissions necessary.

Additional authority should require explicit delegation.

Privilege escalation should remain temporary.

Every escalation becomes an audit event.

---

# Permission Inheritance

Permissions inherit through organizational hierarchy.

Organization

↓

Department

↓

Team

↓

Workspace

↓

Object

↓

Action

The most restrictive applicable permission always prevails.

---

# Auditability

Every important action produces an immutable audit record.

Audit records include:

Actor.

Timestamp.

Action.

Object.

Previous State.

New State.

Reason.

Execution Source.

Correlation ID.

Audit exists for accountability.

Not surveillance.

---

# Non-Repudiation

Every important business action should remain attributable.

Users should not be able to deny authenticated actions.

Digital accountability protects organizational trust.

---

# Privacy

Organizations own organizational data.

Individuals own personal preferences.

Artificial Intelligence processes information only within granted permissions.

Privacy boundaries remain explicit.

---

# Data Ownership

Business data always belongs to the organization.

SHUNYA provides stewardship.

Not ownership.

Organizations should retain control over:

Export.

Deletion.

Retention.

Archival.

Migration.

Access.

Ownership remains portable.

---

# Encryption

Sensitive information should remain encrypted:

In transit.

At rest.

During backup.

Where appropriate, sensitive application secrets should remain encrypted in memory only for the duration necessary.

Encryption is infrastructure.

Not business logic.

---

# Secrets

Secrets include:

Passwords.

API Keys.

Certificates.

Private Keys.

OAuth Tokens.

Webhook Secrets.

Secrets should:

Rotate safely.

Remain external to source code.

Remain auditable.

Never appear in logs.

---

# Compliance

The architecture should support compliance frameworks without redesign.

Examples include:

GDPR.

SOC 2.

ISO 27001.

Industry-specific compliance.

Compliance is achieved through architecture.

Not documentation alone.

---

# Governance

Governance preserves architectural integrity.

It defines:

Responsibilities.

Decision authority.

Ownership.

Approval chains.

Policy enforcement.

Governance exists to maintain consistency as organizations grow.

---

# Governance Levels

Strategic Governance.

Organizational Governance.

Operational Governance.

Project Governance.

Object Governance.

Each level possesses clearly defined responsibilities.

---

# Policy Engine

Policies define organizational behavior.

Policies may govern:

Approvals.

Retention.

Security.

Notifications.

Automation.

Financial thresholds.

Access.

Compliance.

Policies remain configurable.

Architecture remains unchanged.

---

# Delegation

Authority may be delegated.

Responsibility remains attributable.

Delegation records:

Delegator.

Delegate.

Scope.

Duration.

Reason.

Approval.

History.

Delegation never removes accountability.

---

# Separation of Duties

Critical business operations should support separation of duties.

Examples:

Requester ≠ Approver.

Creator ≠ Auditor.

Initiator ≠ Reviewer.

Financial controls remain enforceable through architecture.

---

# Organizational Roles

Roles define responsibility.

Examples:

Administrator.

Leadership.

Manager.

Contributor.

Reviewer.

Auditor.

Observer.

Roles define capability.

Permissions define access.

---

# Risk Governance

Risk should be continuously governed.

Risk categories include:

Operational.

Financial.

Legal.

Compliance.

Security.

Knowledge.

Relationship.

Execution.

Artificial Intelligence should continuously surface emerging governance risks.

# Change Governance

Every significant change should preserve:

Architecture.

Security.

Compliance.

Business continuity.

Knowledge.

Trust.

# Data Governance

Owner.

Steward.

Classification.

Retention Policy.

Access Policy.

Compliance Scope.

Quality Expectations.

Data governance protects organizational intelligence.

---

# Information Classification

Information should support classification.

Examples:

Public.

Internal.

Confidential.

Restricted.

Highly Restricted.

Classification influences:

Permissions.

Retention.

Encryption.

Audit.

Sharing.

---

# Data Retention

Organizations define retention rules.

Retention should consider:

Business value.

Compliance.

Operational needs.

Historical knowledge.

Legal obligations.

Expired information should archive before deletion whenever appropriate.

---

# Business Continuity

Organizations should remain operational despite:

Infrastructure failure.

Employee absence.

Cyber incidents.

Integration failures.

Unexpected growth.

Continuity is an architectural responsibility.

---

# Disaster Recovery

Recovery objectives should define:

Recovery Time Objective (RTO).

Recovery Point Objective (RPO).

Backup Frequency.

Validation Frequency.

Recovery Testing.

Recovery procedures should be tested.

Not assumed.

---

# Backup Philosophy

Backups exist to restore organizations.

Not databases.

Recovery should preserve:

Objects.

Relationships.

Events.

Knowledge.

Memory.

Permissions.

History.

Backups without validation create false confidence.

---

# Security Monitoring

The platform continuously monitors:

Authentication anomalies.

Permission anomalies.

Unexpected access.

Configuration drift.

Integration failures.

Infrastructure health.

Monitoring supports prevention.

Not only incident response.

---

# Incident Management

Security incidents should follow:

Detection.

Classification.

Containment.

Investigation.

Resolution.

Recovery.

Reflection.

Learning.

Every incident should improve resilience.

# Governance Success

Governance succeeds when:

Permissions remain understandable.

Compliance becomes natural.

Recovery remains reliable.

Growth remains sustainable.

Trust compounds over time.
